The Plan

Fast Lane is behind us. Everything below works forward from the first meeting after fall break to three qualifiers: Roadrunner on November 14, Saguaro Blossom on January 23 and Copper on February 6.

Three qualifiers, two robots

The strategy is deliberately unromantic: build something that scores first, then design something better while it is already competing.

Why the Starter Bot first. A rookie team that spends October designing has nothing to drive in November. A rookie team that builds REV’s robot in October has a scoring machine, a season of match data, and a very well-informed opinion about what to design next. Half the team builds Mk-1; the other half prototypes Mk-2 from the first meeting back.

Two things to settle during fall break

Neither is optional, and both fail quietly if they are left to the first meeting back.

1. The Expansion Hub. A Control Hub has four motor ports. Mecanum uses all four. The Starter Bot’s intake and flywheel are motors five and six, so Mk-1 needs an Expansion Hub and a second Control Hub will not substitute. We own one from last season, marked as flaky. BA-17 now tests it before it is trusted: update its firmware in the REV Hardware Client first — stale firmware is the usual cause — then run every port and wiggle every cable under load. We only need two good ports, so one dead port is survivable; a hub that drops the whole link is not. If it drops even once, a replacement (REV-31-1153, about $275) has to be ordered, and the old one becomes the bench spare.

2. Inventory the parts library against the bill of materials. We have the parts from an earlier season’s Starter Kit V3.1 plus the team’s own library, so most of the superstructure should already be on the shelf. What is worth checking early, because it is consumable and easy to be short of: 15mm extrusion (the guide eats 420mm x9 and 225mm x5), polycarbonate sheet (REV-41-7537), 8 intake flaps, 4x 90mm grip wheels and 4x 1in soft grip wheels, 4x 90T and 1x 60T gears, #25 chain and sprockets, 2x Smart Robot Servo V2, a Core Hex motor, one spare UltraPlanetary + HD Hex for the flywheel, and M3 hardware in bulk — the guide pre-loads 195 screws and 195 nyloc nuts before step one. BA-11 is that inventory, and it is the first card back from break for a reason: anything short has to be ordered that same day.

Ten meetings to Roadrunner

Five weeks back from break, two meetings each. The scheduling fact that makes it fit: four of the five Starter Bot assemblies are bench work — launcher floor, intake, flywheel and feeder, and pollen pusher all get built off the robot, so four crews can build them at once. If you fall behind. The one date that cannot move is BA-16 finished by Oct 23. Everything after it is programming and practice, and programming compresses; building does not. If week two slips, drop the pollen pusher (BA-15) — the robot scores without it. If week three slips, go to Roadrunner with TeleOp and no autonomous. A robot that drives and shoots badly beats a robot that stays in the pit.

Hub accounting. The team has two Control Hubs: one lives on the programming board, one is on the student robot. That covers both tracks and neither ever gets unbolted to run a meeting. The Expansion Hub above is a third board, not a replacement for either — it hangs off the student robot’s Control Hub to carry motors five and six.

Stickers & awards

When a card is stamped, everyone who worked on it marks one box in that category. Three boxes earns that category’s sticker. Awards are handed out at the last meeting before each break, not the moment they are earned. Stickers are printed by the team; the one exception is FULL STACK, an embroidered patch for the team shirt.
